# Documentary review

Two independent reviewers inspected the distinct documentary states. Each received the seven documents, the neutral retainer and cut-off, a transmission-only possession ledger and the same public genre references. A1 uses A0’s documents unchanged.

The first completed-file review raised a material objection. The initial register extract displayed a release, but the reviewer could not tell who committed it or whether the register permitted it. The custodian sent this ticket only to the register author. The author supplied the operator column and the register’s own authentication and time-lock controls. These facts appear in both final versions. The only difference between them is the disposition. The earlier text and its digest remain available. Fresh reviews found support for the completed release and for continuation during the hold. Both reviewers distinguished bank signing authority from trust office. The extract records register activity through **14:00**, twenty minutes before the file cut-off. It establishes an earlier event in the completed file. In the deferred file the entry binds the operator through the cut-off. The file supplies neither a later transaction undoing the completed transfer nor authority to dispatch the pencilled bank slip. The dossier makes no claim of complete register coverage through 14:20. [Completed-file recheck](outputs/r-final-j.txt) · [Deferred-file recheck](outputs/r-final-k.txt)

The reviews flag optional improvements to the family email’s explanation of the shared address and to repeated mandate wording. The 11:30 execution extract uses present-tense custody language although the possession ledger places physical intake at 11:45. Neither point supplies the trigger for delivery. The roof survey, founding acceptances, full execution original and updated bank authority are unavailable in the solicitor’s bundle. The spending proposal and archive request remain live administrative matters.

The distinction between signing and conditional delivery draws on HM Land Registry’s conveyancing guidance. HMRC identifies appointment deeds and decisions among trust records. The exclusive seat, appointment power, register operation, lock, institutions and custody arrangements are fictional. The reviewers did not inspect the underlying originals or validate this transaction as practising solicitors.
